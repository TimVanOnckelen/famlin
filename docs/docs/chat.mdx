# Family chat

Chat gives each enabled family a shared conversation for quick messages that do not need to become a feed post. It is a family group chat, not a private one-to-one inbox.

Open **Chat** from the app navigation and select a family if you belong to more than one. You can send text or attach a photo or video, and reply to a particular message to keep a conversation in context. New messages appear automatically while chat is open; an unread badge helps you spot activity when you are elsewhere in the app.

Chat is available in the web and mobile apps when the family administrator has enabled it for that family. Only members of that family can access its chat.

## Encrypted private messages

Private one-to-one messages sent from the mobile app are end-to-end encrypted: only you and the other person can read them, not the server or its administrator. You don't need to set anything up — the app creates your keys the first time you open a private conversation.

- A small lock under a message means it is encrypted. **Not encrypted** means it was sent without encryption, for example from the web app (which can't encrypt), or before the other person had opened a private conversation in the app. A banner above the message box tells you whether your next message will be encrypted, and why not if it won't be.
- Either person can turn encryption off for a conversation from the conversation menu. Both of you then see a note in the conversation, and you can turn it back on the same way.
- The web app shows encrypted messages as a lock with a note to open the conversation in the app.
- Encrypted conversations support text and photos; videos can't be sent in them yet.

### New phone

Your keys are saved automatically in your phone's own keychain backup: iCloud Keychain on iPhone, your Google backup on Android. On a new phone signed in to the same Apple or Google account (and on Android, restored from that backup), your old messages are readable again without doing anything.

Moving between an iPhone and an Android phone, or with that backup turned off, you have two other options:

- **Scan from your old phone.** On the new phone, choose **Restore your encrypted messages → Scan from my other phone**, then on the old phone go to **Profile → Encrypted messages → Link a new device** and scan the code.
- **Recovery code.** Under **Profile → Encrypted messages** you can create a recovery code for extra safety. Keep it somewhere safe, such as a password manager. Nobody, including your administrator, can recover it for you.

If none of these work, choose **Start over**: new messages work right away, and the other person still has your whole conversation.

To check that nobody has swapped the keys, compare the **safety code** with the other person in person (from the conversation menu), or scan each other's code.
